# Orca workflow, Claude-only adaptation: review, 2026-09-24

Scope: the branch `claude/orca-workflow-improvements-f2e8f2` over `develop` (commits `6487e23` and `3c9c656`), which adapts the Studio Igor process in [gamestudio/](../../gamestudio/README.md) to one provider on one Claude subscription: model pins per role, Codex and Kimi removed, raster images out of workers' scope, a root [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) with Karpathy's guidelines adapted to the studio. Four reviewers ran in parallel, read-only, each with one lens: vulnerabilities (the shell instruments, the prompt-injection surface, secrets, git safety), lack of idea (what the Claude-only switch forces but the branch left implicit), common sense (cross-document consistency and every claim the branch introduced), and weak spots (window arithmetic, single points of failure, the QA plan). Every finding was checked against the files before anything changed; the instruments were run after the edits (`bash -n`, a full `health-check.sh` run, `work-check.sh` on this worktree, `ui-diff-check.sh` up to its engine check).

## Verdict

The model pins and the Codex removal were correct and complete; the branch's own defects were in what it left unsaid. Four HIGH items: every landing step and three instruments still targeted `main` while this repository's trunk is `develop`; the 5-hour session window, shared by every worker and the Producer, is what bounds a wave, and no rule named the sign or the cure when it fills mid-wave; a sentence in CLAUDE.md gave task cards, which workers write, authority over the Producer's spec with no list of what a worker may never do; and the raster decision contradicted the art pipeline in GAME.md, which needs an image model in two of its three steps. Everything above 90 % confidence and mechanical was applied (22 items across 13 files); three decisions and one class of pre-existing inconsistencies are listed below.

## Decisions taken by the owner, 24 September 2026

1. **QA: the driver and the judge are split.** The QA role, batch table and playtest rules assumed a single-player, seed-deterministic 2D game: five parallel playtests, one scenario and seed each, "is it boring, is losing fair" asked of the tester. This game needs 3–8 clients per round, its ownership sync is not seed-reproducible, and a Three.js canvas gives Haiku only screenshots to judge; five playtests × three clients is fifteen Chromiums on the same machine as the wave. Applied: Haiku drives the headless test clients GAME.md already plans through scripted scenarios and reports numbers (desyncs, FPS, fish delivered, mines, time to the first grab); "fun and fair" is answered at the friend-group milestone playtests, or by one Sonnet 5 scenario on request; parallel playtests are capped by Chromium count, two until measured; this game's scenario cut is written with the first headless clients. Files: roles/qa-tester.md, STUDIO.md §4, agents.md.
2. **The art direction changed instead of an owner lane.** GAME.md's pipeline was AI sketches → procedural skeleton → AI detail, two steps of image-model work no worker can do. The owner chose to change the pipeline rather than produce those steps by hand: concept as text and SVG → procedural skeleton → detail by code (palette shaders, procedural patterns, SVG decals, Blender renders through MCP), with a decision-log row and the art risk reworded. The `asset-` lane added earlier in this review was dropped again. Files: GAME.md, STUDIO.md §5, agents.md, roles/ui-developer.md.
3. **The machine stays as it is.** Three changes the risk reviewer recommended were declined: `DISABLE_AUTOUPDATER=1` in the Claude Code environment, an effort floor in the user's Claude Code settings, and removing the stale `claude` 2.1.156 under `~/.nvm`. The docs carry the compensating rules instead: update between waves only, `--effort` on every launch, the stale copy named in agents.md.

## Applied by a standing rule, not a new decision

Landing step 3 now reads "a PR into `develop`, squash-merged, source branch deleted": the repository's own git flow since 23 September. The pilot landed by local merge into trunk; if the owner prefers that speed for hobby waves, the step is one sentence to revert.

## Estimates, not measurements

The risk reviewer's window arithmetic (a pilot wave of 12 launches moved the weekly window by about 2 pp; a week holds 8–35 session windows; so one wave may take anywhere from a tenth of a session window to all of it before `xhigh` and playtests) is an estimate from the pilot's numbers and is recorded only to justify the measure-first rule in START_PROMPT.md. Anthropic publishes no token figures for the windows; the first wave here is the measurement.
